    How to read the clock

    A Jewish day begins at sunset. The night is divided into 12 hours, and the day, from sunrise to sunset, into another 12. These are sha'os zmaniyos: each is one twelfth of the darkness or the daylight, so they stretch in summer and shrink in winter.

    On the dial the day's hours (gold) run across the top, from sunrise on the left to sunset on the right, the way the sun crosses the sky. The night's hours (blue) run underneath. The marker on the rim is where you are now.

    Each hour belongs to one of the twelve months and its tribe: the first hour to Nisan and Yehudah, the second to Iyar and Yissachar, and so on. The thin colored band shows each tribe's flag. Tap any hour to meet it.

    The thin inner ring is the ordinary clock, bent to fit. Jewish times like "3:00 of the day" count hours and minutes from sunrise (or from sunset at night) in these stretched hours.

    Times are calculated for sea level and may differ from your shul's luach by a minute or two. Don't rely on them to the last minute.

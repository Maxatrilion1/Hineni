Zmanim

    How to read the clock

    The day is divided into 12 hours from sunrise to sunset, and the night into 12 from sunset to sunrise. These are sha'os zmaniyos: they stretch in summer and shrink in winter.

    The outer ring holds the 24 hours. The day (gold) runs across the top from sunrise on the left to sunset on the right, like the sun across the sky. The night (blue) runs underneath. The Jewish day begins at the right, at sunset.

    The inner ring is the ordinary clock, bent to fit. Where its hours bunch up, Jewish hours are short; where they spread, Jewish hours are long. The dots are the zmanim.

    Times are calculated for sea level and may differ from your shul's luach by a minute or two. Don't rely on them to the last minute.
